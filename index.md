## Biography

We face complexity when there is no clear guideline for action. Today’s world is characterized by complexity. Diverging political interests, technological disruptions, and societal upheavals have created competing and divisive expectations about what we should be doing in our personal lives and in society.

My research examines the complexity of markets and how people and organizations manage it. I study questions such as how public organizations respond to market pressures, how organizations contribute to society while satisfying shareholder interests, and how consumers reshape markets.

My current projects examine markets for coffee, tattoos, sneakers, fashion, music, transportation, microdosing, and education, as well as social projects in Africa, South America, and Southeast Asia. Based on my students’ theses, I also study digital influence, online influencers, and the acculturation of wealthy consumers.

My work has been published in leading journals including the *Journal of Consumer Research*, *Journal of Marketing Research*, *Journal of Marketing*, *Academy of Management Journal*, *Journal of Retailing*, *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, and *International Journal of Research in Marketing*. It has been presented at the World Economic Forum, the Association for Consumer Research, the European Association for Consumer Research, Consumer Culture Theory, and the American Marketing Association.

My research has also been featured in media such as CBC, Radio-Canada, CTV News, Global TV, Channel News Asia, *Les Affaires*, *Le Devoir*, and the *National Post*.

I have received close to $1M in funding from organizations including the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council and the Fonds Société et Culture.

I currently serve on the editorial review boards of:
