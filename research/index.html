Research

I study how consumers and firms deal with market complexity, the conflicting expectations that institutions, technologies and social norms place on them, and how they use that complexity to shape markets. For scholars, my work explains how market categories form and change, how newcomers gain access to elite categories, and how new brands resolve cultural contradictions to build distinctive identities. For managers, it addresses core questions of strategy: how to enter a category, how to build a distinctive and resonant position, and how to shape the market you compete in. I build in-depth case studies from archives, interviews and fieldwork, often in partnership with the organizations involved, from Brazilian Jesuit schools to AAK's shea sourcing program in West Africa. The cases range from streetwear and craft coffee to the creator economy and the regulation of beer at Brazil's 2014 World Cup.
